Rule analysis

Flags chains of two binary comparisons connected by 'and' where the middle operand is identical, suggesting simplification to a chained comparison.

Fix: Rewrites 'left op middle and middle op right' into 'left op middle op right', preserving the operators and operands exactly as they appear.

10 hazards
  • If the middle operand has side effects (e.g., a function call), evaluating it once in the chained form vs twice in the original changes behavior.
  • If the middle operand is an overloaded property with __get__ side effects, repeated access differs from single access.
  • In chained comparisons, Python evaluates the middle operand only once; if the original relied on two evaluations (e.g., for mutation detection), semantics break.
  • If the middle operand is a mutable expression accessed via indexing or attribute, and the first comparison mutates it, the second comparison sees the mutated value—chaining prevents this intermediate state.
  • If the operators are not identical (e.g., '<' and '>'), the rule does not fire, but misidentification could occur if shape matching is flawed.
  • If the expressions are parenthesized for grouping (e.g., '(x < y) and (y < z)'), the fix may incorrectly strip parentheses needed for precedence in larger expressions.
  • If the chain involves non-transitive operators (e.g., 'is' with '=='), the chained form may not reflect the original logic due to differing associativity.
  • If the middle operand is a lambda or comprehension whose evaluation order affects scoping or side effects, reducing evaluations alters behavior.
  • If comments are attached to the 'and' token or middle operand, the rewrite may discard them during AST reformatting.
  • If the code relies on short-circuiting of 'and' for error suppression (e.g., 'a and b.c where b might be None'), chaining removes this short-circuit, potentially raising AttributeError.
