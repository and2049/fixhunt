Rule analysis

Flags non-empty tuple literals used as subscription indices when parentheses are present but disallowed by configuration.

Fix: Removes the outer parentheses around the tuple literal inside the subscription brackets, preserving inner elements and formatting exactly as written.

10 hazards
  • Rewriting `(expr,)` as `expr,` changes a parenthesized expression into a tuple, altering meaning when `expr` is not a tuple-forming construct.
  • If the tuple contains a starred expression like `*(xs,)`, removing parentheses may invalidate syntax or change unpacking behavior.
  • In delayed evaluation contexts (e.g., lambdas or comprehensions inside the subscript), stripping parentheses could bind commas differently, altering structure.
  • If the tuple appears in an annotation-like context not recognized by Ruff’s typing heuristic (e.g., string annotations or conditional imports), the fix may be applied inappropriately.
  • When the tuple is part of a larger expression due to line continuation (e.g., backslash), removing parentheses may break syntactic grouping.
  • If the tuple contains a walrus operator (`:=`), removing parentheses may change the scope or binding behavior of the assigned variable.
  • Comments inside the parentheses (e.g., `(# x , 1)`) may be lost or misplaced if the rewrite does not preserve comment attachment.
  • In nested subscriptions like `d[(1, 2)][(3, 4)]`, fixing only the outer tuple may produce invalid or misleading intermediate expressions if inner tuples are also problematic.
  • If the subscript is part of a slice object construction via `__getitem__`, altering the tuple form may change how the slice is interpreted by the object’s implementation.
  • When the tuple is generated via a macro, decorator, or codegen tool that relies on the parentheses for parsing, removal may break tooling even if runtime behavior is preserved.
