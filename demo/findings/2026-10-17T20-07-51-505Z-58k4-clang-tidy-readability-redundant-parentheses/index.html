Rule analysis

The rule flags unnecessary parentheses around expressions where they do not affect precedence or meaning.

Fix: Removes parentheses that are not required for operator precedence or to disambiguate parsing, rewriting the expression to its form without the redundant grouping.

12 hazards
  • Removing parentheses around a function-like macro invocation may change how arguments are grouped if the macro expects comma-separated parameters.
  • Stripping parentheses from a cast-expression like `(T)x` alters the cast target and may cause misinterpretation as a declaration or incorrect type conversion.
  • Eliminating parentheses around a braced-init-list such as `(T){x}` can prevent aggregate initialization and instead invoke a constructor if available.
  • Deleting parentheses in a lambda-expression like `([]{})()` may prevent it from being treated as a single postfix-expression, breaking the call.
  • Removing parentheses around a comma-expression such as `(a, b)` changes evaluation order context, potentially altering which value is used in larger expressions.
  • Taking away parentheses from a conditional-expression like `(a ? b : c)` can lead to incorrect parsing due to lower precedence of `?:`, changing the bound of the ternary.
  • Erasing parentheses around an assignment expression like `(x = y)` may prevent it from being treated as a single value in outer contexts, affecting use in conditions or function calls.
  • Stripping parentheses from a `sizeof` or `alignof` operand like `sizeof (T)` risks misparsing if followed by a declarator, turning it into an ambiguous or invalid declaration.
  • Removing parentheses around a unary operator expression such as `(!p)` can alter binding with adjacent operators, e.g., in `!p && q` vs `!(p && q)`.
  • Deleting parentheses in a pack expansion like `(Args)...` breaks the expansion syntax, causing a compile error.
  • Taking away parentheses around a dependent name in a template, such as `(typename T::type)`, may hinder lookup and break instantiation.
  • Stripping parentheses that visually group code for readability may drop associated inline comments attached to the parentheses lines, reducing code clarity.
