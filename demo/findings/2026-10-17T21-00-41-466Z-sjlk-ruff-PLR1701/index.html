Rule analysis

The rule flags repeated isinstance() checks on the same variable combined with logical OR operators.

Fix: Replace a chain of isinstance(var, T1) or isinstance(var, T2) or ... with isinstance(var, (T1, T2, ...)) for Python <3.10, or isinstance(var, T1 | T2 | ...) for Python >=3.10 when target-version allows it.

10 hazards
  • Rewriting isinstance(var, T1 | T2) fails at runtime if T1 or T2 is a tuple, e.g., T = (int, str); isinstance(x, T | float) raises TypeError.
  • If the variable in isinstance checks is not identical (e.g., due to shadowing or aliasing), merging changes which object is tested.
  • Side effects in isinstance arguments (like function calls) are evaluated once instead of per clause, altering behavior if those functions have side effects.
  • Comments embedded within the isinstance chain (e.g., isinstance(x, # type int) or ...) may be lost during rewrite due to token reformatting.
  • In nested expressions, precedence changes may occur if the original chain relied on 'or' short-circuiting with non-boolean contexts.
  • When used in annotation contexts or stringized annotations, the rewritten form may not be syntactically valid or may change meaning.
  • If the variable is a property with a getter, merging reduces getter calls from N to 1, altering side effects or state-dependent behavior.
  • Macros or decorators that rely on the syntactic form of multiple isinstance calls may malfunction after rewrite.
  • In eval() or exec() contexts with restricted namespaces, the tuple or union form may resolve names differently than sequential lookups.
  • If one isinstance check uses a forward reference as a string and others don't, the merged tuple/union form may inconsistently handle string resolution.
