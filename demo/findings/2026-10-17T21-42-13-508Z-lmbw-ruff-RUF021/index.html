Rule analysis

Flags chains of 'and'/'or' without explicit precedence via parentheses.

Fix: Inserts parentheses around subexpressions to enforce 'and' before 'or' precedence, e.g., rewriting 'a or b and c' as 'a or (b and c)' and 'd and e or f' as '(d and e) or f'.

11 hazards
  • Rewriting 'a or b and c' as 'a or (b and c)' changes evaluation if 'a' has side effects and is truthy, as 'b and c' would no longer be evaluated.
  • Rewriting 'd and e or f' as '(d and e) or f' changes evaluation if 'd' is falsey and 'e' has side effects, as 'e' would now be evaluated in the original but not in the fix due to short-circuiting.
  • If 'b' or 'e' is a property with a getter that raises an exception, moving its evaluation inside parentheses may cause the exception to be raised in different contexts.
  • In lambdas, adding parentheses may alter binding precedence if the lambda body is part of a larger expression, though rare.
  • Comments placed between operators (e.g., 'a # comment\n or b # comment\n and c') may be lost or misplaced during rewrite if the tool does not preserve comment attachment.
  • If the expression is part of a tuple or list unpacking, adding parentheses could be interpreted as a tuple literal, altering structure (e.g., 'x, y = a or b and c' vs 'x, y = a or (b and c)' — same here, but risky in nested contexts).
  • In default argument values, rewriting could affect when side effects occur due to early binding, though the fix doesn't change timing relative to function definition.
  • If 'a', 'b', or 'c' are overloaded operators (e.g., __or__, __and__) in a custom class, adding parentheses does not change semantics as these are still method calls, but evaluation order of operands is preserved — low risk, but noted for completeness.
  • In async contexts, if 'b' or 'e' is an awaitable, rewriting does not change await behavior as the fix doesn't introduce or remove awaits — but if mistakenly wrapped in a non-async context, could cause issues; however, the fix preserves expression structure so this is unlikely.
  • If the code uses a linter or formatter that expects specific spacing around operators, the autofix may produce formatting that conflicts with project style, requiring reformat.
  • In string annotations or forward references (rare for expressions), parentheses could be misinterpreted, though this applies only to annotations, not executable code.
